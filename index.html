AI in Your Practice

From your first prompt to your firm’s next workflow.

Two days of practical AI learning for design professionals and the people who lead them. Daniel Tal, Jenn Becker, and guest presenters take you from your first prompt to a plan for your firm, using real project examples throughout.

Reserve your spot See what you’ll learn View the agenda

01 / What you’ll learn

Six skills, each taught in the sessions that follow.

Every session shows the whole job: the task, the inputs, the process, the result, the review, and the limits. Start simply, build context, test the result, and keep people accountable.

For landscape architects, architects, planners, designers, visualization professionals, educators, firm owners, and practice leaders.

  1. 01

    Start with the fundamentals

    Understand what a large language model is and why its answers need checking, write a prompt that works, and pick the right tool for the task, the data, and the evidence you need.

  2. 02

    Research and write with AI

    Go from a question and source material to a reviewed draft or proposal, checking every claim against its source along the way.

  3. 03

    Prompt images and animation

    Turn sketches, massing models, and site captures into concept imagery and short animations without losing design intent.

  4. 04

    Build models and small tools

    Generate editable 3D models from a prompt, photo, sketch, or CAD file, and describe your way to a small custom tool you test before you trust.

  5. 05

    Use AI responsibly

    Decide what data can enter a tool, who reviews the output, and how use is disclosed, and weigh the energy and infrastructure questions behind adoption.

  6. 06

    Bring AI into your firm

    Learn from a university studio and specialist practice, see how agents take on coordinated work, and leave with a 30-day pilot plan for your own firm.

Agenda · Boulder 2027

The agenda

Tap a session for its abstract and outcomes. Each one is tagged with the skill it teaches. All times local to Boulder.

01

Day 1, 9 a.m.–4 p.m.: Foundations, generative design, 3D modeling, and responsible practice

  1. 9:00–9:15 Welcome: What we want you to leave knowing15 minSpeakers: Daniel Tal + Jenn Becker
  2. 9:15–10:00
    01Fundamentals S01 · 45 min · Beginner
    AI fundamentals: From LLMs to your first useful prompt Speakers Daniel Tal + Jenn Becker

    A plain-language introduction to large language models and the difference between generating an answer and establishing a fact. Build a simple prompt, improve it with context, and discuss when an answer needs verification or a different tool.

    Learning outcomes
    • Explain an LLM without technical jargon.
    • Write a task/context/constraints/output prompt.
    • Recognize uncertainty and plausible errors.
  3. 10:00–10:30
    01Fundamentals S02 · 30 min · Beginner
    Choosing your tools: Chat, deep research, and evidence Speakers Daniel Tal + Jenn Becker

    Compare common AI assistants and research modes through the work they support. Show when a quick conversation is enough, when deeper research is useful, and how to inspect sources rather than accept a confident summary.

    Learning outcomes
    • Choose a tool for a task.
    • Distinguish chat from a research workflow.
    • Inspect citations and source quality.
  4. 10:30–11:00
    02Research & writing S03 · 30 min · Beginner to intermediate
    Research, writing, and proposals: From question to reviewed draft Speakers Daniel Tal + Jenn Becker

    Follow a practical sequence from a research question and source material to an outline, written draft, and proposal narrative. Show where human editing, evidence checks, and project-specific judgment change the output.

    Learning outcomes
    • Structure a repeatable writing workflow.
    • Review claims against sources.
    • Adapt a draft to a real audience and project.
  5. 11:00–11:15 Break15 min
  6. 11:15–noon
    03Images & animation S04 · 45 min · Beginner to intermediate
    Generative design, part 1: Image prompting for concepts and renderings Speakers Jenn Becker

    Turn sketches, massing models, Google Earth captures, and draft renderings into client-ready imagery. Build a detailed image prompt with AI, use segmentation to keep design intent, and compare each iteration against what the design actually is.

    Learning outcomes
    • Write an image prompt that tells the story of the design.
    • Use AI to draft and refine prompts for an image model.
    • Separate conceptual imagery from verified project information.
  7. noon–12:45 Included lunch and conversation45 minOne included meal; placement proposed
  8. 12:45–1:15
    03Images & animation S05 · 30 min · Intermediate
    Generative design, part 2: Video prompting and AI animation Speakers Jenn Becker

    Chain a massing model, diagrams, and AI key frames into a short animation. Storyboard the sequence, prompt each transition, and judge where a fast AI animation can stand in for a full rendering pass and where it cannot.

    Learning outcomes
    • Storyboard an animation from key frames.
    • Prompt transitions between frames.
    • Judge an AI animation against design intent and accuracy.
  9. 1:15–2:00
    04Models & tools S06 · 45 min · Intermediate; accessible demonstration
    3D modeling with AI: From photo, sketch, and CAD to a working model Speakers Daniel Tal

    Watch current AI models build editable 3D models from a prompt, a photograph, a hand sketch, or a CAD file, then follow the review that has to happen next: scale, geometry, context, and whether the model is usable downstream.

    Learning outcomes
    • Identify where AI can start or speed up a model.
    • Distinguish imagery from usable model data.
    • Define checks before a model goes into a deliverable.
  10. 2:00–2:15 Break15 min
  11. 2:15–3:00
    05Responsible use S07 · 45 min · All levels
    Responsible practice: Firm policy, data, and human review Speakers Daniel Tal + Jenn Becker

    Translate responsible AI use into everyday firm decisions: what information can enter a tool, who reviews the output, when use is disclosed, and how teams document accountability. Connect a practical policy framework to realistic work scenarios.

    Learning outcomes
    • Identify data boundaries.
    • Define review and escalation responsibilities.
    • Outline an acceptable-use policy.
  12. 3:00–3:30
    05Responsible use S08 · 30 min · All levels
    AI infrastructure: Data centers, energy, and growth Presenter TBD

    Examine the physical infrastructure behind AI and the questions it raises for energy demand, water, land use, communities, and growth. Separate measured conditions from estimates and projections, and discuss why comparisons need context.

    Learning outcomes
    • Interpret the scope of an infrastructure claim.
    • Distinguish forecasts from measured data.
    • Ask better questions about environmental impacts.
  13. 3:30–4:00
    04Models & tools S09 · 30 min · Introductory demonstration
    Coding with AI: From a useful idea to a tested small tool Speakers Daniel Tal

    Show how a non-specialist can describe a useful small tool, work through an AI-assisted draft, and test whether the result does what was intended. Explain what still requires technical review and maintenance.

    Learning outcomes
    • Turn a problem into a clear brief.
    • Iterate using observable failures.
    • Recognize when code needs expert review.

Questions

Do I need previous AI experience?+

No. The program begins with basic concepts and simple prompts before moving into more advanced workflows and firm-level decisions.

Is this only for designers?+

The examples draw heavily on design and professional practice, while research, writing, policy, and implementation sessions apply across many organizations.

Is this a hands-on workshop?+

The proposed format combines presentations, demonstrations, case studies, and questions. Any guided exercises or software requirements will be announced with the final program.
